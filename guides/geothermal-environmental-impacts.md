# The environmental impacts of geothermal energy, measured.

Geothermal power has one of the smallest environmental footprints of any electricity source. Over its whole life it emits a median 37 grams of CO2-equivalent per kilowatt-hour, against 486 for natural gas and 1,001 for coal, by the 2021 review of the National Laboratory of the Rockies. Its local impacts are gases carried up in the fluid, water use, small induced earthquakes and, where fluid is drawn out and not replaced, sinking ground. Each has a working control: scrubbers, reinjection, dry cooling and seismic monitoring.

Figures are quoted from the EIA, the National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR, formerly NREL), the World Bank's ESMAP programme, the USGS, peer-reviewed papers and company statements, each named where it appears. Work-per-kilogram figures are Zemanium model results, labelled [MODEL].

01 / Summary

## What are the environmental impacts of geothermal energy?

| Impact | What causes it | How large | How plants control it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Greenhouse gases | carbon dioxide dissolved in the reservoir fluid, plus building the plant and drilling | median 37 g CO2e/kWh over the life cycle (NLR) | binary plants and full reinjection keep the gas underground |
| Air pollutants | hydrogen sulfide in the steam | about 97% less sulfur compounds than fossil fuel plants (EIA) | scrubbers |
| Water use | evaporation in cooling towers and fluid lost in the reservoir | 5 to 505 gallons per MWh by plant type (Macknick and colleagues) | dry cooling and reinjection |
| Induced earthquakes | injection, withdrawal and stimulation change the stress on small faults | mostly small; the largest at The Geysers is about magnitude 4.5 (USGS) | seismic monitoring and injection limits |
| Sinking ground | pressure falls when fluid is drawn out and not replaced | about 15 m at the centre of the Wairakei bowl, New Zealand, by 2009 | reinjection to hold reservoir pressure |
| Natural features | drawing on the same hot water that feeds geysers and hot springs | site-specific | legal protection at places such as Yellowstone (EIA) |

Direct use of geothermal heat and geothermal heat pumps have almost no negative effects on the environment, by the EIA's account. The impacts in this guide are those of geothermal power plants.

02 / Emissions

## How much CO2 does geothermal energy emit?

A median 37 grams of CO2-equivalent per kilowatt-hour over the whole life cycle: building the plant, drilling, operation and decommissioning. That sits beside solar and far below fossil fuels in NLR's 2021 review of published life-cycle studies:

| Source | Median life-cycle emissions (g CO2e/kWh) |
|---|---|
| Coal | 1,001 |
| Oil | 840 |
| Natural gas | 486 |
| Solar photovoltaic | 43 |
| Geothermal | 37 |
| Concentrating solar | 28 |
| Hydropower | 21 |
| Wind | 13 |
| Nuclear | 13 |

Operating emissions depend on the field, because the carbon dioxide comes from the rock and the fluid, not from burning anything. The World Bank's ESMAP programme reports a global weighted average of 122 g/kWh from a 2001 survey of operating plants, and measured 400 to 1,300 g/kWh at plants in Turkey. Binary plants pass the heat through a closed heat exchanger and inject all the fluid back underground, so they have no air emissions, according to the EIA. Across the fleet, geothermal plants emit about 99% less carbon dioxide than fossil fuel plants, by the EIA's count.

03 / Pollution

## Does geothermal energy cause pollution?

Very little. A geothermal plant burns no fuel, so it makes no smoke, soot or ash. The pollutant to manage is hydrogen sulfide, a gas in the steam that smells of rotten eggs. Plants remove it with scrubbers, and the EIA puts geothermal plants at about 97% less of the sulfur compounds that cause acid rain than fossil fuel plants.

The water that comes up carries dissolved minerals and salts. Most plants inject the steam and water they use back into the earth, which keeps those minerals underground, renews the resource and further reduces emissions, according to the EIA.

04 / Water

## How much water does a geothermal power plant use?

It depends on the plant type and its cooling. The review of operational water consumption by Macknick and colleagues gives these medians:

| Plant | Cooling | Water consumed, gallons per MWh (median) | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Geothermal flash | dry | 5 | one study |
| Geothermal flash | cooling tower | 15 | 5 to 361 |
| Geothermal binary | dry | 270 | one study |
| Geothermal binary | hybrid | 461 | 221 to 700 |
| Enhanced geothermal | dry | 505 | 290 to 720 |
| Natural gas combined cycle | cooling tower | 205 | 130 to 300 |
| Nuclear | cooling tower | 672 | 581 to 845 |
| Coal | cooling tower | 687 | 480 to 1,100 |

A flash plant with a cooling tower consumes a fraction of the water of a gas, nuclear or coal plant with the same cooling. Enhanced systems also need water to stimulate the rock, which is why they sit at the top of the geothermal range.

05 / Earthquakes

## Does geothermal energy cause earthquakes?

It can cause small ones. At The Geysers in California, the withdrawal of steam and the injection of cold water induce small earthquakes that workers and nearby residents often feel. The USGS reports that seismicity there has been fairly stable since the mid-1980s, that the largest quake recorded at the field is about magnitude 4.5, and that much larger ones are thought unlikely because no large continuous fault runs through the field.

Stimulating hot rock for an enhanced system carries the larger risk. In November 2017 a magnitude 5.5 earthquake struck Pohang, South Korea; Ellsworth and colleagues showed in 2019 that stimulation at an enhanced geothermal pilot triggered it. Projects now follow induced-seismicity protocols such as the US Department of Energy's of 2012: screen the site, monitor in real time and adjust or stop injection when quakes pass agreed thresholds. Fervo Energy publishes its own approach to managing induced seismicity.

06 / Ground

## Can geothermal energy make the ground sink?

Yes, where fluid is drawn out faster than it is replaced and reservoir pressure falls. At Wairakei in New Zealand, which has produced power since 1958, the centre of the main subsidence bowl had sunk about 15 m by 2009, at a peak rate of 498 mm a year in 1979, by the account of Brockbank and colleagues. Injecting the fluid back holds reservoir pressure up, and most geothermal plants now do it, according to the EIA.

07 / Heat pumps

## Geothermal heat pumps and the environment.

Geothermal heat pumps move heat between a building and the ground through sealed loops of pipe. They can use 25% to 50% less energy than air-source heat pumps, by the US Department of Energy's guide, and the EIA counts them among the geothermal uses with almost no negative effects on the environment. Our guide to [closed-loop geothermal](https://zemanium.com/guides/closed-loop-geothermal) explains the loop types.

08 / New designs

## How newer geothermal designs change the footprint.

- Closed-loop systems circulate their own fluid through sealed wells and exchange no water with the rock by design. At Geretsried, Germany, Eavor's first loop lost 0.5% to 2% of its circulating fluid to the rock, and the company expects about 0.1% in deeper, tighter basement rock.
- Enhanced systems open fractures in hot dry rock, which raises water use and calls for seismic monitoring. More in our [enhanced geothermal guide](https://zemanium.com/guides/enhanced-geothermal-systems).
- Hotter fluid does more work per kilogram. At 400 °C a kilogram of fluid can deliver 566 to 617 kJ of work, against 172 kJ at 200 °C, so a plant on 400 °C fluid lifts and reinjects less than a third as much fluid for the same work. That is the case for [superhot rock geothermal](https://zemanium.com/guides/superhot-rock-geothermal).

09 / Questions

## Common questions.

### What are the environmental impacts of geothermal energy?

Low carbon emissions, a median 37 g CO2e per kWh over the life cycle, and four local impacts: hydrogen sulfide in the steam, water use of 5 to 505 gallons per MWh by plant type, small induced earthquakes and, where fluid is not reinjected, sinking ground.

### Does geothermal energy cause pollution?

Very little. Plants burn no fuel, scrub hydrogen sulfide from the steam and emit about 97% less sulfur compounds and 99% less carbon dioxide than fossil fuel plants, according to the EIA. Binary plants have no air emissions.

### Is geothermal energy clean?

Yes. Its median life-cycle emissions of 37 g CO2e per kWh are close to solar photovoltaic at 43 and about a thirteenth of natural gas at 486, by NLR's 2021 review.

### Does geothermal energy cause earthquakes?

Sometimes, and mostly small ones. The largest recorded at The Geysers in California is about magnitude 4.5, according to the USGS. Stimulation at an enhanced geothermal pilot triggered the magnitude 5.5 Pohang earthquake in South Korea in 2017, and projects now monitor seismicity and limit injection.

### How much water does geothermal energy use?

From about 5 gallons per MWh for a dry-cooled flash plant to 505 for an enhanced system, by the review of Macknick and colleagues, against 672 for a nuclear plant and 687 for a coal plant with cooling towers.

### What are the problems with geothermal energy?

For the environment: gases in the steam, water use, induced earthquakes and sinking ground, controlled by scrubbers, dry cooling, monitoring and reinjection. For the business: high upfront drilling cost and the risk of dry wells, both covered in our [pros and cons guide](https://zemanium.com/guides/geothermal-energy-pros-and-cons).
