# Geothermal wells: how deep they go, what they cost and how long they last.

Geothermal power wells reach 1.5 to 2.5 kilometres for a typical conventional plant and 3 to 7 kilometres for deep enhanced geothermal, by the national laboratories' cost baseline. Drilling one costs millions of dollars: Fervo Energy's Cape Station wells fell from $9.4 million to $4.8 million each. They produce for decades, while the water they bring up slowly cools; in Nevada's public record the median field ran about 1.9 °F cooler each year.

Drilling figures come from the National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR, formerly NREL) and company statements, labelled as such. Field temperatures are measured and operator-reported to the Nevada Division of Minerals; yearly rates are computed by Zemanium from that record.

01 / In brief

## Geothermal wells at a glance.

- Depth: NLR's representative conventional plants draw on 175 °C water at 1.5 km and 225 °C water at 2.5 km; deep enhanced geothermal targets 3 to 7 km. The EIA notes some geothermal wells reach about 2 miles.
- Cost: drilling makes up 30% to 57% of a new plant's capital cost. Fervo's Cape Station wells cost $346 to $634 per foot drilled, against $995 and $1,107 at its Project Red pilot.
- Speed: the fastest of the first six Cape Station wells took 20 days for 13,289 feet. Fervo reported 19,448 feet in 21 days at its Sawtooth 7 well in 2026.
- Life: NLR's cost baseline assumes 30 years for plant and reservoir. The Geysers in California has produced power since 1960.
- Cooling: in 11 of 18 long-running Nevada fields the produced water is more than 5 °F cooler than in its first recorded year.

02 / Depth

## How deep are geothermal wells?

As deep as the heat requires. Geothermal power plants need water or steam at 300 to 700 °F, according to the EIA, and how far down that sits depends on the local geology. Recent wells with public records:

| Well | Measured depth | Temperature | Drilling time | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GDC-36, The Geysers, California (2023) | 9,000 ft (2,743 m) | steam above 475 °F (246 °C) | 69 days | NLR 2025 |
| Project Red pair, Nevada | 11,211 and 11,220 ft (3.42 km) | 376 °F (191 °C) maximum | 59 and 72 days | NLR 2025 |
| Utah FORGE 16B(78)-32 (2023) | 10,947 ft (3.34 km) | research site | 75 days | NLR 2025 |
| Cape Station, Utah, 14 horizontal wells (2023 to 2024) | 13,272 to 15,347 ft (4.0 to 4.7 km), laterals about 4,700 ft | 444 °F (229 °C) maximum | 20 to 34 days for the first six | NLR 2025 |
| Sawtooth 7, Utah (2026) | 19,448 ft (5.9 km) with a 7,500 ft lateral | about 238 °C rock | 21 days | company statement |
| IDDP-2, Reykjanes, Iceland | logged at about 4,550 m | 426 °C | research well | World Geothermal Congress paper |

Measured depth is the length of the hole. Horizontal wells such as Cape Station's run sideways for thousands of feet at their target, so their vertical depth is shorter than their measured depth.

03 / Cost

## How much does it cost to drill a geothermal well?

Millions of dollars per well, and falling. NLR's 2025 analysis of Fervo's wells, using data Fervo supplied:

| Well | Total depth | Days | Feet per day | Cost per foot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Project Red 1 | 11,220 ft | 71 | 158 | $994.62 |
| Project Red 2 | 11,211 ft | 58 | 193 | $1,106.74 |
| Cape Station 3 | 13,316 ft | 33.75 | 312 | $633.45 |
| Cape Station 4 | 13,272 ft | 25.91 | 440 | $471.59 |
| Cape Station 5 | 13,601 ft | 24.26 | 504 | $395.71 |
| Cape Station 6 | 13,289 ft | 20.1 | 567 | $346.45 |
| Cape Station 7 | 13,949 ft | 23.71 | 472 | $394.08 |
| Cape Station 8 | 13,734 ft | 22.27 | 539 | $357.36 |

At $346.45 a foot, Cape Station 6 cost about $4.6 million to drill, before completion. Fervo itself reported its Cape Station well cost falling from $9.4 million to $4.8 million. NLR attributes the gains to diamond (PDC) bits, drilling practices that remove the limits on speed one by one, and drilling several wells from one pad, and its 2025 cost curves sit 12% to 24% below the 2017 baseline for vertical wells and 18% to 26% below for horizontal ones.

Casing and cementing alone make up 30% to 40% or more of a well's cost, according to the US Department of Energy. And not every well finds what it is drilled for: NLR's baseline assumes 60% of exploration wells at a new conventional site succeed.

What well costs do to the price of power is in our [cost guide](https://zemanium.com/guides/geothermal-electricity-cost), and the [LCOE calculator](https://zemanium.com/lcoe-calculator) turns any capital cost into dollars per megawatt-hour.

04 / Lifespan

## How long do geothermal wells last?

Decades. NLR's cost baseline assumes a 30-year life for plant and reservoir, and The Geysers in California has produced electricity since 1960, according to the EIA. Over that time the water a field produces cools as injected water draws heat from the rock, and operators drill makeup wells to hold output.

Nevada publishes the monthly temperature of every producing geothermal well. We tracked 18 fields with ten or more years of record; ten of them, from fastest cooling to warming, show the spread:

| Field (regulator code) | Years | First-year mean | 2025 mean | Change per year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blue Mountain (BLU) | 2009 to 2025 | 368 °F | 281 °F | -5.4 °F |
| WRS2 (WRS2) | 2015 to 2025 | 261 °F | 212 °F | -4.9 °F |
| Jersey Valley (JVP) | 2012 to 2025 | 331 °F | 292 °F | -2.9 °F |
| McGinness Hills 2 (MGH2) | 2015 to 2025 | 336 °F | 307 °F | -2.9 °F |
| Brady Hot Springs (BHS) | 2012 to 2025 | 280 °F | 248 °F | -2.5 °F |
| McGinness Hills (MGH) | 2012 to 2025 | 335 °F | 306 °F | -2.2 °F |
| Steamboat (STB) | 2012 to 2025 | 290 °F | 263 °F | -2.1 °F |
| Patua (PTA) | 2013 to 2025 | 303 °F | 293 °F | -0.8 °F |
| Dixie Valley (DXV) | 2012 to 2025 | 330 °F | 330 °F | 0.0 °F |
| Stillwater (STW) | 2009 to 2025 | 270 °F | 297 °F | +1.7 °F |

The median of all 18 fields is a change of about -1.9 °F per year. A field's mean also moves when operators add or retire wells, so these figures describe the produced water, not the rock alone. All 18 fields are in our guide [is geothermal energy renewable](https://zemanium.com/guides/is-geothermal-energy-renewable), and every yearly value is in [one JSON file](https://zemanium.com/data/nevada-field-temperature.json), CC BY 4.0.

The clearest single well-pair record is Fervo's Project Red, which went from 347 °F at its 2024 plateau to 316 °F in July 2026. Our [Project Red tracker](https://zemanium.com/fervo-project-red) scores a forecast of it, registered on 19 November 2026, against the regulator's record every month.

05 / Integrity

## What fails first in a geothermal well.

The steel and cement. A well heats when it produces and cools when cold water is pumped in or the well is shut in, and each cycle stresses the casing and its couplings. A 2018 review of 20 high-temperature wells found that most well failures came from thermal stress during quenching or the start of production. Iceland's IDDP-2 had its production casing damaged between 2,307 and 2,380 m, presumably by repeated heating and cooling. Downhole electronics stop working near 210 to 225 °C.

That is our field. We rebuilt IDDP-1's production casing in a model and ran it through the well's recorded history, and we are building Pandora, a casing joint for wells at 450 to 500 °C. The [superhot rock guide](https://zemanium.com/guides/superhot-rock-geothermal) has the record and the sources.

06 / Heat pumps

## Geothermal heat pump wells are a different thing.

A home or office geothermal heat pump uses the steady temperature a few hundred feet down; it makes no electricity. For a vertical system, holes about 4 inches across are drilled about 20 feet apart and 100 to 400 feet deep, according to the US Department of Energy. The shallowest power well in the table above goes more than 20 times deeper.

07 / Questions

## Common questions.

### How deep is a geothermal well?

For power plants, typically 1.5 to 2.5 km for conventional hydrothermal sites and 3 to 7 km for deep enhanced geothermal, by the NLR baseline. Recent wells range from 9,000 feet at The Geysers to 19,448 feet of measured depth at Fervo's Sawtooth 7 in Utah. Heat pump boreholes are 100 to 400 feet.

### How much does a geothermal well cost?

Fervo Energy reported its Cape Station wells falling from $9.4 million to $4.8 million each. NLR's analysis puts those wells at $346 to $634 per foot drilled, and drilling at 30% to 57% of a new geothermal plant's capital cost.

### How long does a geothermal well last?

Wells and plants run for decades; NLR's baseline assumes 30 years, and The Geysers has produced since 1960. The water they produce cools over time: the median long-running Nevada field ran about 1.9 °F cooler each year, and operators add makeup wells to hold output.

### How long does it take to drill a geothermal well?

Weeks. The fastest of Fervo's first six Cape Station wells took 20 days for 13,289 feet, down from 58 to 72 days for its Project Red pilot wells, and the company reported 19,448 feet in 21 days in 2026. The Geysers GDC-36 well took 69 days for 9,000 feet.

### How hot is the water in a geothermal well?

Power plants use water or steam at 300 to 700 °F, according to the EIA. Cape Station's production test ran at about 195 °C (383 °F), and research wells in Iceland have met fluid above 400 °C.
