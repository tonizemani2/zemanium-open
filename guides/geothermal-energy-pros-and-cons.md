# Geothermal energy pros and cons, with the numbers behind each.

Geothermal power runs day and night: US plants ran at a 65.9% capacity factor in 2025, against 24.0% for solar and 33.8% for wind. It emits about 99% less carbon dioxide than fossil plants and burns no fuel. Its drawbacks are a high up-front drilling bill, a limited number of naturally hot, wet sites, reservoirs that cool over a plant's life, and earthquakes from stimulating rock.

Each point carries its source. Capacity factors are measured by the US Energy Information Administration; costs are Lazard and NLR estimates; Nevada field temperatures are measured and operator-reported to the state regulator, and the field averages are computed by Zemanium.

02 / Advantages

## What are the advantages of geothermal energy?

### It produces power around the clock

Heat underground does not depend on sun or wind. In 2025 the US geothermal fleet ran at a 65.9% capacity factor, meaning it produced 65.9% of what its available capacity could have delivered running flat out all year. Utility solar ran at 24.0%, wind at 33.8% and nuclear at 91.1%, by the EIA's count. Lazard models new geothermal plants at 80% to 90%.

### It emits very little

Geothermal plants burn nothing. The EIA puts their emissions at about 99% less carbon dioxide and about 97% less sulfur compounds than fossil fuel plants. Binary-cycle plants pass the hot water through a heat exchanger and send it back underground, so they produce no air emissions at all.

### The fuel is free and the price is fixed

Once the wells are drilled the heat costs nothing, so geothermal power sells on long fixed contracts. Public US agreements signed between November 2019 and June 2025 priced at $67.50 to $99 per megawatt-hour for 10 to 30 years, as compiled by the National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR, formerly NREL).

### Plants run for decades

The first geothermal power plant ran in Italy in 1904, and The Geysers in California has produced electricity since 1960, according to the EIA. NLR's cost baseline assumes a 30-year life for plant and reservoir.

### The resource is enormous once drilling gets cheaper

Natural hot-water systems are rare, but hot rock is everywhere at depth. The US Department of Energy's Enhanced Geothermal Shot analysis estimated about 7,500 GW of enhanced geothermal potential in the United States, against about 2,700 MW of geothermal capacity running in 2025. Our guide to [enhanced geothermal systems](https://zemanium.com/guides/enhanced-geothermal-systems) explains how that rock is put to work.

03 / Disadvantages

## What are the disadvantages of geothermal energy?

### Most of the money is spent before the first megawatt-hour

Lazard's 2026 estimate for a new geothermal plant is $5,135 to $6,635 per kilowatt, against $1,250 to $1,850 for utility solar. Drilling accounts for 30% to 57% of the capital cost, by NLR's 2025 analysis. That makes the price of geothermal power sensitive to interest rates; our [cost guide](https://zemanium.com/guides/geothermal-electricity-cost) shows by how much.

### Wells can come up dry

A geothermal field is proven only by drilling into it. NLR's 2025 baseline assumes 60% of exploration wells at a new hydrothermal site succeed, in line with a global average of 59% recorded in 2013. Each miss costs millions of dollars.

### Natural sites are limited

A conventional plant needs heat, water and permeable rock in the same place. That combination sits mostly near tectonic plate edges, and most US plants are in western states and Hawaii, according to the EIA. Enhanced and closed-loop systems are built to work without the natural water and cracks.

### Reservoirs cool during a plant's life

Cold water injected back into the rock cools it over time. Nevada publishes the temperature of every producing well each month. Across 18 fields with ten or more years of record, the flow-weighted produced temperature fell by more than 5 °F in 11, held within 5 °F in 3 and rose in 4. At Blue Mountain it fell from 368 °F in 2009 to 281 °F in 2025. Fervo's Project Red well pair went from 347 °F in 2024 to 316 °F in July 2026. Operators answer with new wells, which is a running cost. The field-by-field table is in our guide [is geothermal energy renewable](https://zemanium.com/guides/is-geothermal-energy-renewable).

### Stimulating rock can trigger earthquakes

Enhanced systems open fractures with water under pressure, and that can set off earthquakes. In November 2017 a magnitude 5.5 earthquake struck Pohang, South Korea, injuring 135 people; a government-appointed research committee concluded it was triggered by stimulation at an enhanced geothermal pilot. Fervo Energy's 2026 Department of Energy award in Idaho includes a high-temperature geophone array to monitor this.

### Some plants release gases

Geothermal fluid carries hydrogen sulfide and small amounts of carbon dioxide. Plants use scrubbers to remove the hydrogen sulfide, and most inject the steam and water back into the ground, according to the EIA.

05 / Outlook

## Which disadvantages new technology is working on.

- Drilling cost. Fervo Energy reported its Cape Station wells falling from $9.4 million to $4.8 million each, and NLR's 2025 cost curves cut 12% to 26% from the 2017 baseline. More in our [guide to geothermal wells](https://zemanium.com/guides/geothermal-wells).
- Location. Enhanced geothermal reached commercial power at Cape Station, Utah, on 24 September 2026, by Fervo's account. Eavor's closed-loop plant at Geretsried, Germany, has sent power to the grid since December 2025.
- Power per well. Wells in superhot rock above 375 °C could deliver 30 to 50 MW each against about 10 today, according to ARPA-E. Their steel casing is the limit; that is our work, described in the [superhot rock guide](https://zemanium.com/guides/superhot-rock-geothermal).
- Cooling. Wider, even flow through more fractures uses more rock and slows cooling. Our [Project Red tracker](https://zemanium.com/fervo-project-red) scores a registered forecast of one well pair against the regulator record every month.

The companies doing this work are listed in our [guide to geothermal companies](https://zemanium.com/guides/geothermal-companies).

06 / Questions

## Common questions.

### What is the biggest disadvantage of geothermal energy?

Up-front cost. Drilling makes up 30% to 57% of a new plant's capital cost, and the whole plant is paid for before it sells power. Lazard puts new geothermal at $5,135 to $6,635 per kilowatt, several times the cost of utility solar per kilowatt.

### What is the main advantage of geothermal energy?

It delivers clean power day and night. US geothermal plants ran at a 65.9% capacity factor in 2025, against 24.0% for solar and 33.8% for wind, while emitting about 99% less carbon dioxide than fossil plants.

### Is geothermal energy bad for the environment?

Its effects are small next to fossil fuels. Plants emit about 99% less carbon dioxide and 97% less sulfur compounds, scrub hydrogen sulfide and reinject their water. The local risks are induced earthquakes from stimulation and the land and water used at the site.

### Why is geothermal energy not used more?

Conventional plants need rare spots where heat, water and permeable rock meet, and drilling is expensive and risky. Enhanced and closed-loop systems remove the need for natural water and cracks, and their drilling costs fell sharply between 2022 and 2025.

### Can geothermal energy run out?

The Earth's heat will not run out, but a single reservoir can cool when heat is drawn faster than the rock refills it. In Nevada, 11 of 18 long-running fields produced cooler fluid in 2025 than in their first recorded year.

### Does geothermal energy cause earthquakes?

Stimulating rock for enhanced systems can trigger earthquakes. A government-appointed committee linked the magnitude 5.5 Pohang earthquake of 2017 in South Korea to stimulation at an enhanced geothermal pilot. Fervo Energy's 2026 Department of Energy award in Idaho includes a high-temperature geophone array to monitor seismicity.
