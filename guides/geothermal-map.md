# Geothermal map: where the Earth's heat is closest to the surface.

These maps show where geothermal heat lies shallowest, from 51,882 heat-flow measurements on land in the IHFC Global Heat Flow Database 2024. The world map shows measured heat flow. The maps of the United States and Europe turn it into the depth at which rock reaches 200 °C, where flash power plants start, with our conduction model. Nevada has the shallowest heat of any US state: 200 °C at about 4.4 km at its median heat flow. In Europe, Iceland leads at 3.6 km.

Heat flow is measured, compiled by the International Heat Flow Commission. Depths are Zemanium model results on that data, labelled [MODEL]. Outlines are from Natural Earth. Every map is free to reuse under CC BY 4.0 with credit to Zemanium.

01 / World

## World geothermal map: measured heat flow.

![World geothermal map of measured heat flow on land, median per one-degree cell; the highest values in orange run along the western United States, Iceland, the East African Rift, Indonesia, Japan and New Zealand, and old continental interiors in grey run lowest](https://zemanium.com/assets/charts/geothermal-heat-flow-map.webp)

Median heat flow in every one-degree cell of land with a measurement, IHFC Global Heat Flow Database 2024. Squares mark the 166 cells holding a value above 250 mW/m². Blank land is unmeasured. Measured; free to reuse under CC BY 4.0 with credit to Zemanium.

Heat flow is the heat escaping through each square metre of ground, in milliwatts per square metre (mW/m²): the temperature gradient measured in a borehole times the conductivity of the rock. The median of the world's measured continental cells is 59 mW/m², which puts 200 °C at about 8.5 km by our model. The hottest belts follow plate boundaries, rifts and young volcanoes: the western United States, Iceland, the East African Rift, Indonesia, Japan, New Zealand and the rim of the Pacific, where the EIA notes most major geothermal resources sit. The squares mark convective hot spots, where hot water rising through the rock carries heat faster than conduction alone.

The shallow heat of the United States covers the Great Basin of Nevada and western Utah, the Snake River Plain of Idaho, Oregon and California. That is where the country's geothermal power plants are: most are in western states and Hawaii, and California leads in geothermal electricity, according to the EIA. Nevada publishes the output of every plant; our [power plant guide](https://zemanium.com/guides/geothermal-power-plants) lists all of them.

Squares mark 155 half-degree cells with a value above 250 mW/m² in the map's frame, all but one west of the 104th meridian. The Atlantic coast and Great Lakes states in the table, measured in fewer places, run cooler: 200 °C lies 9.1 km down or deeper at their medians.

Europe's shallowest heat lies in Iceland, Tuscany, the Upper Rhine Graben between France and Germany, the Paris and Molasse basins, Hungary's Pannonian Basin and western Turkey. 05 / Method

## How to read these maps.

- Heat flow is measured. Each cell shows the median of the values inside it: one-degree cells on the world map, half-degree cells on the regional maps.
- Depth is modelled. We solve heat conduction down from the surface with rock conductivity of 2.5 W/m/K, 1 µW/m³ of heat produced in the top 10 km and a surface at 12 °C. A conductivity of 2.2 to 3.0 W/m/K moves the world median depth to 200 °C between 7.4 and 10.3 km, as our [geothermal gradient guide](https://zemanium.com/guides/geothermal-gradient) shows.
- Values above 250 mW/m², 2,023 of them, mark places where moving water carries the heat. Conduction does not describe them, so they are drawn as squares and left out of the depths.
- Blank land is unmeasured, not cold.

The world map of depth to 150, 200, 250 and 400 °C, and the share of land within reach of each drilling depth, are in the gradient guide. What the heat is worth in a power plant is in our [power plant guide](https://zemanium.com/guides/geothermal-power-plants).

06 / Questions

## Common questions.

### Where is geothermal energy found in the United States?

Mostly in the West. Nevada, Idaho, California, Oregon, Colorado, Utah and Arizona reach 200 °C within about 6.2 km at their median heat flow, by our model, and most US geothermal power plants are in western states and Hawaii, according to the EIA.

### Which US state has the most geothermal energy?

Nevada has the shallowest heat, with 200 °C at about 4.4 km at its median heat flow of 109 mW/m². California generates the most geothermal electricity, according to the EIA, led by The Geysers.

### Where is geothermal energy found in Europe?

In Iceland, Tuscany, the Upper Rhine Graben, the Paris and Molasse basins, Hungary's Pannonian Basin and western Turkey, where 200 °C lies between 3.6 and 6.1 km at the regional median, by our model.

### What does a geothermal map show?

Usually heat flow, the heat escaping through the ground in mW/m², or the temperature at a given depth. Our maps show measured heat flow worldwide and the modelled depth to 200 °C in the United States and Europe.

### How deep do you have to drill for geothermal energy?

For electricity, to rock of about 110 °C or hotter for a binary plant and about 200 °C for a flash plant, by NLR's baseline. At the state median, 150 °C lies at 3.2 km in Nevada and 7.3 km in Texas, and much shallower at local hot spots, by our model.
